# Germany Tech Jobs — Practical Guide

A focused reference for **software engineers** (especially **frontend / full-stack**) targeting roles in **Germany**. Not legal or tax advice — verify visa rules and contracts with official sources or a lawyer.

---

## Why Germany (for tech)

- Large EU market with strong **automotive**, **fintech**, **SaaS**, **cloud**, and **enterprise** hiring.
- Many teams work in **English**, especially in Berlin, Munich, Hamburg, and international companies.
- **Blue Card EU** path exists for qualified non-EU professionals (salary thresholds apply; updated yearly).
- Remote/hybrid is common, but **on-site or hybrid in Germany** is often required for visa-sponsored roles.

## Work authorization (overview)

| Situation | Typical path |
|-----------|----------------|
| **EU citizen** | Work without Blue Card |
| **Non-EU, job offer** | **EU Blue Card** (min salary threshold; IT often qualifies) or other residence title |
| **Job seeker visa** | Short-term search visa (rules vary; check Ausländerbehörde) |
| **Student → work** | Graduate visa options after German degree |

**Official sources:**

- [Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/) — government portal for skilled workers  
- [Federal Office for Migration](https://www.bamf.de/) — residence topics  

**Blue Card (conceptual):** Employer sponsors; minimum gross salary (threshold changes yearly — check current figure for “general” vs shortage occupations). IT specialists are commonly in demand.

## Salary (gross, annual — rough 2025 bands)

Ranges vary by city, company size, and level. **€ = gross per year** before tax.

| Level | Frontend / full-stack (typical) |
|-------|----------------------------------|
| Junior (0–2 y) | €45k – €58k |
| Mid (3–5 y) | €58k – €72k |
| **Senior (5+ y)** | **€72k – €95k** |
| Staff / lead | €90k – €115k+ |

**Munich** and **big tech** often pay **10–20% above Berlin** for same level. Startups may offer **lower base + equity**.

Always clarify: **gross vs net**, **13th month (Weihnachtsgeld)**, **bonus**, **VSOP/ESOP**, **30 vacation days** (common in DE contracts).

## German hiring expectations

### CV (Lebenslauf)

- **1–2 pages** PDF; clear reverse-chronological experience  
- **Photo optional** (still seen in DE; not required in tech)  
- Include: **location**, **work authorization** (e.g. “Eligible for Blue Card”), **LinkedIn**, **GitHub** if relevant  
- **English CV** is fine for international teams; **German CV** helps for local Mittelstand  

### Cover letter

- Shorter than US “essays”; **half page** tailored to company  
- Mention: why Germany, why this product, **parallel product experience** (platform + storage + infra UI) if relevant  

### Interview process (typical)

1. Recruiter screen (30 min)  
2. Hiring manager / team fit  
3. Technical: live coding or take-home (React/TS common)  
4. System design (senior roles) or architecture discussion  
5. Culture / values  
6. Offer → negotiation → **reference checks** (sometimes)  

**Culture notes:** Punctuality, direct communication, clear documentation — align with your Rakuten work on **ESLint, MSW, a11y docs**.

## Relocation basics (first months)

| Topic | Tip |
|-------|-----|
| **Registration (Anmeldung)** | Register address at Bürgeramt within deadline after moving |
| **Health insurance** | Mandatory; employer often registers you (public or private) |
| **Bank account** | Needed for salary; N26, Deutsche Bank, etc. |
| **Tax class (Steuerklasse)** | HR/payroll setup with employer |
| **Probation (Probezeit)** | Often **6 months** in German contracts |

## Red flags in job posts

- No mention of **visa support** when you need it  
- “Unpaid trial” or vague equity-only compensation  
- Role requires **C1 German** but post is English-only (misaligned)  
- Extremely broad “full stack + DevOps + mobile” at senior level (scope creep)
